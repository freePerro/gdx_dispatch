# Credit-card surcharge on the customer pay page, off by default

**Date:** 2026-09-16
**Status:** **PLAN** — being built on `feat/card-surcharge-pay-page`; flip to `MERGED #N` in the PR that lands it, `RELEASED vX.Y.Z` after the prod walk. The fee stays **off** in production until Doug has given Stripe (the acquirer) Visa's 30-day written notice and turns the setting on.
**Branch:** `feat/card-surcharge-pay-page`
**Rulings (Doug, 2026-09-16):** rate 2.9%; the fee is shown before the customer commits; the page says bank transfer has no fee; booking per the recommendation below.

## What already exists (do not rebuild)

- **Stripe enrollment.** The account is already enrolled in Stripe's surcharging public preview. Probed 2026-09-16 in test mode with API version `2026-03-25.preview`: a Visa or Mastercard credit PaymentMethod returns `amount_details.surcharge.status = available` with `maximum_amount` = 3% of the purchase; a Visa debit PaymentMethod returns `unavailable`, maximum $0; a 5% attempt on credit is refused ("A surcharge amount can only represent at most 3.0% of the original purchase amount for Visa credit"). Stripe does the funding-type exclusion and the network cap; we do not re-implement either. (<https://docs.stripe.com/payments/cards/surcharge>, read 2026-09-16.)
- **The one-intent pay page** (#742, v1.121.1): `create-intent` with `method`, `_create_usable_intent`, the M16 in-flight gate, the M12 sweep, the webhook that labels the rail from the PaymentMethod that paid, and the audit rows. The surcharge rides the card branch of that page; ACH is untouched.
- **Ledger posting** (`modules/ledger`): `build_payment_lines` debits the method's account (card → 1050 Undeposited Funds by default) and credits 1200 AR; `seed_coa` tops up system accounts **by role on every run**, which is how a new role reaches existing installs; `reverse_entry` reverses a whole entry, so a void reverses every leg.
- **Billing terms** (`modules/billing_terms`): tenant-level fee configuration on `tenant_settings` with an audited upsert (`billing_terms_updated`) and a Settings page block. The surcharge rate lives there.
- **Stripe receipts** display the surcharge separately on their own (Stripe doc, "Receipts").

## The rules this has to satisfy (read 2026-09-16)

- **Minnesota** Minn. Stat. § 325G.051 (2025 statutes): a credit-card surcharge is allowed up to 5% with a conspicuous notice on the checkout page; a discount for paying by cash, check "or similar means" is not a surcharge. § 325D.44 (2025) puts mandatory fees into advertised prices; the Attorney General's FAQ treats a card fee the customer can avoid as exempt — so a fee-free method must genuinely exist on the page. Bank transfer is that method, and debit cards carry no fee either.
- **Visa** (U.S. Merchant Surcharge Q&A, Feb 2024): credit only; the lower of 3% and the merchant's cost of acceptance; 30 days' written notice to the acquirer before the first surcharge; disclosed at the point of sale and itemised on the receipt; the amount carried in the network's dedicated field (Stripe populates it).
- **Stripe** (surcharge doc): the top-level `amount` includes the fee and `amount_details[surcharge][amount]` names it; you must show the fee and the total before confirmation and let the customer choose another method; refunds must return the fee, prorated on partial refunds; the merchant is responsible for the legal and network limits.
- **Rate:** 2.9% flat is below Stripe's cost on every ticket (2.9% + 30¢), so it never exceeds the cost of acceptance; Stripe's technical cap of 3% binds first only if a future rate exceeds it.

## Decision: how it works

1. **Setting.** `tenant_settings.card_surcharge_percent` (Numeric 5,4, nullable; `None`/`0` = no surcharge). Editable on Settings → Billing terms, audited like the other terms. Ships `NULL`. Bound 0–0.05 (the state cap).
2. **Two-step card flow when the rate is set.** The page creates a PaymentMethod from the card element (`stripe.createPaymentMethod`), posts it to `create-intent` with `method="card"`. The server creates the intent with that PaymentMethod attached and `amount_details[surcharge][enforce_validation]=enabled` on API version `2026-03-25.preview` (per-request header; the rest of the app stays on its version), reads `surcharge.status` and `maximum_amount`, computes `fee = min(round(balance × rate), maximum_amount)` when available and `0` otherwise (debit), then updates the intent to `amount = balance + fee` with `amount_details[surcharge][amount] = fee` and `metadata.surcharge_cents = fee`. The page shows the breakdown — invoice amount, card fee, total, or "debit card: no fee" — with **Pay** and a **pay by bank transfer instead (no fee)** link, then `confirmCardPayment(client_secret)` confirms the already-attached PaymentMethod. Without a rate the page keeps today's one-step card flow.
3. **Notice.** When the rate is set, a line above the tabs: "Credit cards carry a 2.9% processing fee. Debit cards and bank transfer (ACH): no fee." The ACH tab is labelled "no fee". The invoice email carries the same sentence in its HTML body, placed before the link line so the HTML path (which strips a trailing link line to render its button) keeps it (audit, round 1). Reminders and statements also send pay links and do not carry the notice; the statute requires it on the checkout page, which every link opens — listed as deferred below.
4. **Booking.** `payments.surcharge_amount` (Numeric 12,2, nullable) beside `amount`. At settlement the fee is read from Stripe (`amount_details.surcharge.amount`, one retrieve on the preview version) and wins over the stamp when they differ; the stamp is the fallback when Stripe cannot be read, and a fee larger than the receipt books as payment only, loudly (audit, round 1). `amount` is what settles the invoice; the invoice total is untouched. The ledger debits the bank account for `amount + surcharge`, credits AR for `amount`, and credits the new system account **4950 Card Surcharge Income** (role `SURCHARGE_INCOME`, seeded by role on existing installs). Stripe's own fee is already an expense on the GL side, so the two net on the P&L. The webhook and `/confirm` read `metadata.surcharge_cents` (version-independent; the event payload is rendered in the endpoint's API version and does not carry `amount_details.surcharge`) and record `amount_received − surcharge` as the payment and `surcharge` on the row. The office bell and the invoice's payment history show "plus $X card fee".
5. **Refunds.** A full Stripe refund (Dashboard) voids the payment row through `charge.refunded`, fee and all, but that path has never reversed the payment's ledger entry — the AR leg included — and this change does not alter it; only the office's void route resettles the books (audit round 2 ran both: pre-existing, in `FOUND_NOT_FILED.md`). <!-- untracked by design, see CLAUDE.md "One issue per session"; link-ok --> Partial refunds already leave the payment alone by design (M3); the fee's proration on a partial refund is done in the Dashboard by the office, as Stripe requires, until the refund-reconciliation plan is built. The in-app refund route books cash/check refunds and never talks to Stripe; unchanged.
6. **The Pay step re-runs the gate.** A customer can sit on the breakdown for hours. Before `confirmCardPayment`, the page calls `create-intent` again with the same PaymentMethod: idempotent when nothing changed (same intent back), a 409 with M16's message if a bank transfer for the invoice started meanwhile, and a fresh intent if the balance moved — which the page re-renders and asks the customer to confirm again (audit, round 1: the two-step flow parks a confirmable intent that the one-step flow never had).
7. **Idempotency.** The card key gains the PaymentMethod id (`gdx-pi-{invoice}-card-{amount}-{pm}`): a second card is a new attempt, not a replay with different parameters. `_create_usable_intent`'s "abandoned intent carrying an account" rule (from #742) applies to replays only; an intent this request created with a PaymentMethod on purpose is not cancelled.
8. **The portal's own card route** (`POST /portal/invoices/{id}/pay`) is deleted. Nothing calls it — the portal sends customers to the public pay page — and a second card mint site that could never carry the fee would breach "surcharge consistently". Its presence test is retargeted to assert its absence.
9. **4950 is seeded at first use, not at the next seed run.** "Seeded by role on existing installs" was true only when something ran the seed, and nothing does between a deploy and the next accounting-settings mutation — prod's chart of accounts had no row for the role with posting on (audit round 2, read-only prod query). The line builder now tops the role up (`ensure_role_account`) before it emits the fee leg, so the first surcharged receipt creates 4950 instead of raising inside the recorder with the card already charged. A deactivated or ambiguous owner stays loud: the seed keys on role across inactive rows and never resurrects one.
10. **With a PaymentMethod the mint is the same with or without a rate.** The office can turn the fee off while a customer sits on the breakdown, and the Pay step re-posts the same PaymentMethod under the same key. A mint whose parameters depend on the rate would then be a different request under a used key, which Stripe refuses ("the idempotency layer compares incoming parameters to those of the original request and errors if they're not the same", <https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests>, read 2026-09-16); so an attached card is always probed on the preview version, and only the *sizing* depends on the rate. The replay returns the intent the customer saw: a fee they were shown and agreed to stays on it (disclosure and charge match), and a card that was never sized never gains one (audit round 2). The rate lookup itself goes by ORM primary key, since raw SQL on the dashed tenant id never matches on SQLite (same round).

## Rejected alternatives

- **A fee line on the invoice.** Changes an issued invoice's total after the fact, prints on the PDF as if it were the price of the work, and reads as a mandatory fee under § 325D.44. The fee belongs to the payment, not the sale.
- **Recording the whole Stripe amount as the payment.** Lands as an overpayment: `payment_exceeds_receivable` fires and the excess goes to Customer Credits. Wrong semantics, wrong account.
- **A flat 3%.** Exceeds Stripe's cost on large tickets (2.91% on $2,232), which breaches Visa's cost-of-acceptance rule.
- **Bank-transfer discount instead of a surcharge.** Legally simpler, but Doug chose the surcharge; the discount remains available later without conflict.
- **Stripe's "automatic surcharge" for Checkout.** Private preview and we do not use Checkout.

## Money and rollback

Two nullable columns; existing rows read as no surcharge; downgrade drops both. Ships with the rate `NULL`, so nothing changes for any customer until the setting is turned on. Rollback of the feature is the setting; rollback of the release is a revert.
